Keyword matching looks for specific words or phrases -- legal markings, project codenames, "do not distribute" style language. It's simple, fast, and catches things regex fundamentally can't (there's no "shape" to a sentence like "this is privileged and confidential").

Proximity (context-aware) matching solves a different problem: data that's sensitive but not distinctively formatted. A bare 9-digit number could be an SSN, a tracking number, or a phone extension with the area code squashed in -- there's no way to tell from the number alone. The fix real DLP engines use is requiring supporting context nearby: if "SSN" or "social security" appears within a short window of the number, confidence goes up; if there's no such context, the match is suppressed entirely rather than generating noise.

This is a genuine trade-off: proximity matching will miss a bare SSN with zero surrounding context, but it will also generate far fewer false positives than flagging every 9-digit number in every document.
