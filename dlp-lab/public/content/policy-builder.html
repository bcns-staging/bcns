Detectors on their own don't do anything -- a policy is what turns a detection into a decision. This is where every technique from the pages above gets combined: pick which detectors matter for a given policy, weight them by how much you trust each one, set a threshold, and choose what happens when content crosses it.

How the score works

Each selected detector reports its own confidence (0-1) for a piece of content. The policy's score is the weighted average of those confidences -- so a policy can lean heavily on, say, Exact Data Match (very precise, so weight it high) while barely counting the statistical classifier (broader but noisier, weight it low).

Actions, modes, and exceptions

  • Actions: alert (log only), redact (mask matched spans in place), encrypt (wrap the content in a real AES-256-GCM envelope), quarantine, block, or allow.
  • Monitor mode always allows the content through, but records what the policy would have done -- the safe way to test a new policy before enforcing it.
  • Require justification turns a block into a "policy tip": instead of silently blocking, the end user sees why it was flagged and can type a business justification to send anyway -- logged for later audit rather than blocked outright.
  • Exceptions let specific recipients or users bypass a policy entirely, for known-safe destinations or approved workflows.
