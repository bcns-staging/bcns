Pattern matching is the oldest and still most heavily used DLP detection technique: a regular expression looks for a shape (16 digits, a 9-digit number with dashes, a specific prefix), and where possible, a checksum confirms the match is structurally valid data rather than a coincidental string of digits.

Why checksums matter

A regex for "16 digits" alone would flag phone numbers, order IDs, and random data constantly. Real card numbers, however, satisfy the Luhn algorithm -- a simple checksum built into the numbering scheme itself. Similarly, IBANs use a mod-97 checksum across a rearranged version of the number. Validating these turns a noisy regex into a high-confidence signal.

Try the invalid examples below -- notice they're correctly ignored, not just flagged with lower confidence. That's the difference between "shape matches" and "shape and checksum match."
