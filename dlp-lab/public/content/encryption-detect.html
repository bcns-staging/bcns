A DLP tool that can't decrypt a file fundamentally cannot inspect its content -- there's no clever trick around that. The honest and correct response is to flag it as uninspectable and let policy decide what to do (block it, quarantine it for a human to check, or require it be sent through an approved encrypted channel instead).

Two ways to detect it

  • Structural flags: zip files carry an explicit "this entry is encrypted" bit in their header; PDFs reference an /Encrypt dictionary in their trailer when password-protected. These are unambiguous when present.
  • Shannon entropy: when there's no explicit flag, measuring randomness helps -- genuinely random or encrypted data averages close to 8 bits of entropy per byte, while human text or normal documents sit meaningfully lower. A "document" with entropy near the maximum is a red flag even without a structural marker.

~4.2 bits/B

~6.0 bits/B
Encrypted / random
~8.0 bits/B

Max entropy is 8 bits/byte. No explicit header flag needed -- a file sitting up near the ceiling is almost certainly encrypted or already compressed.
