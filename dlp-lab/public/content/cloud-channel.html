A CASB (Cloud Access Security Broker) sits between your organization and the cloud apps people actually use, and its central idea is that the same content can be perfectly fine or a serious incident depending entirely on where it's going. Uploading a customer export to your company's IT-approved storage is routine. Uploading the same file to a random file-sharing site is data loss.

This is why the sanctioned-apps list matters more here than the policy's own configured action: an unsanctioned destination escalates any detected sensitivity straight to a block, on the theory that there's no acceptable outcome for sensitive data leaving through an unapproved channel -- even if the underlying policy was only configured to "alert."
