Sensitivity labels are metadata, not detection -- a human (or an automated classifier) tags a document as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted, and that tag travels with the document independent of whether any pattern or keyword actually matches its content.

Labels are useful precisely because content inspection can miss things: a document might not contain any SSNs or credit card numbers and still be extremely sensitive (an unreleased product roadmap, merger discussions). Labeling lets policy react to classification as a first-class signal -- e.g., "block any Restricted-labeled document from being uploaded to an unsanctioned cloud app," regardless of what content scanning finds.
