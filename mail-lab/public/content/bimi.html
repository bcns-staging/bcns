BIMI (Brand Indicators for Message Identification) lets a verified brand's logo appear next to their emails in a supporting inbox. It's often mistaken for an authentication mechanism, but it isn't one -- it's a reward for having strong DMARC enforcement already in place.

Requirements to actually display

  • A published BIMI TXT record at default._bimi.yourdomain.com pointing to an SVG Tiny PS logo.
  • DMARC policy of p=quarantine or p=reject -- p=none is not enough, because BIMI would otherwise let a domain gain brand recognition without actually blocking spoofed mail sent in its name.
  • pct=100 -- if the policy only applies to a fraction of mail, the logo doesn't display.
  • Some providers (Gmail) additionally require a Verified Mark Certificate (VMC) from a certificate authority, asserted via the a= tag -- modeled here but not deeply validated.

Try publishing a record with a weak DMARC policy first, then tighten it, and watch the evaluation flip from "will NOT display" to "will display."
