PGP (Pretty Good Privacy) / OpenPGP solves the same problem as S/MIME -- content confidentiality and integrity -- with a completely different trust model: there is no Certificate Authority at all. Instead, you exchange public keys directly with people you communicate with (or rely on a "web of trust" of others vouching for a key), and encrypt/sign using those keys directly.

What's actually happening

  • Encryption uses hybrid cryptography: the message is encrypted with a fast symmetric key, and that symmetric key is itself encrypted with the recipient's RSA public key -- only their private key can unwrap it.
  • Signing lets a recipient verify the message came from you and wasn't altered, using your private key to sign and your public key to verify -- independent of encryption (you can sign without encrypting, or do both).

This lab generates two real keypairs (Alice and Bob) so you can run a full round trip: Alice encrypts and signs a message using Bob's public key and her own private key; Bob decrypts it with his private key and verifies Alice's signature with her public key.
