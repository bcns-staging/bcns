Secure email gateways like Proofpoint's URL Defense or Mimecast's URL Protection rewrite every link in a message to point at their own service first. This buys two things a one-time send-time scan can't:

→
✏️

Replaced with a signed /url-defense?u=...&sig=... link
→
🖱️
User Clicks (later)
Gateway re-checks the real destination right now
→
Benign
Redirect through
Malicious
Block with a warning page
  • Click-time re-checking: a domain that was clean when the email arrived can turn malicious hours or days later (attackers often register domains and let them "age" before weaponizing them). Rewriting means the link is re-scanned every time someone actually clicks it.
  • Visibility: the gateway logs who clicked what and when, useful for incident response after a phishing campaign is discovered.

The wrapped link here is HMAC-signed so it can't be tampered with in transit -- try changing a character in a rewritten link and notice the click handler rejects it as invalid rather than silently redirecting anywhere.
