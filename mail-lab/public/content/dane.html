DANE (DNS-based Authentication of Named Entities) takes a different approach than MTA-STS: instead of trusting the public Certificate Authority system at all, it publishes the mail server's certificate fingerprint directly in DNS as a TLSA record, secured by DNSSEC. A connecting server compares the certificate actually presented during the TLS handshake against the pinned fingerprint.

This is powerful but operationally sharp-edged: if you rotate your TLS certificate (which you should do periodically) without also updating the TLSA record, every DANE-validating sender will suddenly refuse to deliver to you -- correctly treating the mismatch as a possible attack. This lab lets you reproduce that exact failure mode on purpose.

Note: real DANE depends on DNSSEC to prevent an attacker from forging both the fake certificate and a fake TLSA record. This simulation focuses on the certificate-pinning mechanism itself; DNSSEC's role is assumed rather than modeled.
