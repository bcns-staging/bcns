DMARC ties SPF and DKIM together and, crucially, checks them against the address a human actually sees: the From: header. Without DMARC, an attacker can pass SPF and even DKIM using their own throwaway domain while still displaying your CEO's name and address in the From: header the recipient reads.

Alignment: the key concept

DMARC doesn't just check "did SPF pass" or "did DKIM pass" -- it checks whether the domain that passed is the same domain (or organizationally related) as the header-From domain. This is called alignment:

  • Relaxed (default): the organizational domain must match, e.g. mail.example.com aligns with example.com.
  • Strict (adkim=s / aspf=s): the exact domain must match.

DMARC passes if either SPF-aligned-pass or DKIM-aligned-pass is true -- it doesn't require both.

Policy and rollout

The p= tag tells receivers what to do with mail that fails: none (just report it), quarantine (spam folder), or reject (bounce it). pct= lets a domain roll out enforcement gradually -- e.g. pct=50 applies the policy to only half of failing messages (chosen at random per message), letting a domain owner watch for breakage before going to 100%.

rua= is where receivers send aggregate XML reports summarizing pass/fail volumes -- see the DMARC Reports page.
