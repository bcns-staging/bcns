Business Email Compromise (BEC) is consistently one of the costliest categories of email fraud, and it's also the hardest to catch technically: there's usually no malware, no bad link, and often the sending domain authenticates perfectly with SPF/DKIM/DMARC -- because the attacker registered their own domain and set it all up correctly. The attack is pure social engineering.

Detection has to rely on softer signals, combined into a composite score:

  • Does the display name impersonate someone the organization trusts?
  • Does the sending domain look suspiciously close to a trusted brand domain?
  • Does Reply-To quietly redirect responses to a different domain than the one shown in From?
  • Does the language match known fraud patterns -- urgency, secrecy, requests for money transfers, gift cards, or credential changes?

No single signal is proof on its own (a legitimate email can be urgent too), which is why real systems score and combine multiple weak signals rather than hard-blocking on any one of them.
