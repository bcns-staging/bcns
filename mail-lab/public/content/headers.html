Every mail server a message passes through prepends its own Received: header -- never removes or edits existing ones. That means the full header block, read bottom-to-top, is an append-only audit log of the message's actual path. It's also the single most useful thing to check by hand when investigating a suspicious email ("View Source" / "Show Original" in most mail clients).

Three more header fields are frequently confused, and attackers rely on that confusion:

HeaderWhat it actually controls
FromWhat the recipient sees as the sender -- checked by DMARC alignment
Reply-ToWhere a "Reply" actually goes -- not checked by any authentication mechanism
Return-Path (envelope sender)Where bounce/delivery-failure notices go -- checked by SPF

A message can have a perfectly legitimate, DMARC-passing From and still redirect replies to an attacker via Reply-To -- this is a very common tactic in business email compromise.
