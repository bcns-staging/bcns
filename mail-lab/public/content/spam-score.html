Before machine-learning classifiers, spam filtering was almost entirely rule-based: a list of heuristics, each worth some number of points, summed into a score and compared against a threshold. SpamAssassin is the best-known example, with hundreds of rules covering header oddities, content patterns, and reputation. This lab implements a small version of the same idea with a handful of rules: excessive capitalization, exclamation marks, link density, and known spam trigger phrases.

Modern commercial filters layer Bayesian and neural classifiers on top of rules like these, but the rule layer never fully goes away -- it's cheap, explainable, and catches the crude, high-volume spam that doesn't justify spending ML compute on.
