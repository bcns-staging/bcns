SPF breaks on forwarding (the forwarder's IP isn't authorized). DKIM breaks if the forwarder modifies the body or headers at all -- and many legitimate services do: mailing lists add footers or subject tags, some forwarders rewrite headers. That means a perfectly legitimate message can fail DMARC entirely by the time it reaches its final destination, through no fault of the original sender.

ARC (Authenticated Received Chain) solves this by having each intermediary cryptographically attest: "here's what SPF/DKIM/DMARC looked like when I received this, and here's my signature over what I'm now sending you." A receiver can then trust that attestation even if the message's own authentication has since broken.

The three ARC headers

HeaderPurpose
ARC-Authentication-ResultsRecords what SPF/DKIM/DMARC evaluated to at that hop
ARC-Message-SignatureA DKIM-style signature over the message as it's being sent onward
ARC-SealSigns the whole ARC set (and any earlier ones), chaining hops together

Each hop increments an instance number (i=1, i=2, ...).
