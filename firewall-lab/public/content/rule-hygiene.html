Rule bases only grow over time -- rules get added under deadline pressure and rarely get removed, since nobody wants to be the one who deletes something and breaks production. A recurring audit catches three things a security engineer should clean up:

  • Shadowed rules -- a broader, earlier rule makes a later one unreachable (see Shadowed & Redundant Rules).
  • Zero-hit rules -- rules that have never matched any traffic this lab has evaluated. In production this is measured over months, but the idea is the same: a rule nobody's traffic ever uses is a candidate for removal (after confirming it's truly unused, not just rarely hit).
  • Overly permissive rules -- an allow rule with no restriction on protocol or port and no logging enabled. Even if it's scoped to specific zones, an unrestricted service on an unlogged allow rule is a standing risk and an audit blind spot.
